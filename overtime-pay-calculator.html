How overtime pay is figured

Overtime pay is your regular hourly rate multiplied by the overtime multiplier, times the overtime hours. That amount is added to your regular pay.

Overtime rate = Hourly rate × Multiplier
Total pay = Hourly rate × Regular hours + Overtime rate × Overtime hours

Example: At $20 an hour with 40 regular hours and 10 overtime hours at 1.5x, your overtime rate is $30 an hour. Regular pay is $800, overtime pay is $300, and the total is $1,100 before taxes.

When does overtime start?

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, most hourly workers in the United States must be paid at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Some states and some employers also pay overtime after 8 hours in a day, on weekends or on holidays, and some pay double time. Contracts and union agreements can offer more than the legal minimum. This page is for planning and is not legal advice, so check your state labor department or your employer for the rules that apply to you.

Frequently asked questions

Do salaried workers get overtime?

Not always. Many salaried employees are exempt, but that depends on their duties and on a minimum salary level. Being paid a salary does not by itself make someone exempt.

Is overtime taxed more?

Overtime is taxed at the same rates as other income. It can look like a bigger cut because a larger paycheck may push withholding up for that period, but the extra tax is counted against your yearly total.

How do I work out overtime for two weeks?

Overtime is usually counted one workweek at a time, not averaged over two weeks. Run the calculator once for each week and add the results.

How much does a yearly salary equal per hour?

Divide the yearly salary by 2,080 (40 hours × 52 weeks) to get the hourly rate, then use it here. The Hourly to Salary Calculator does that conversion for you.
