How compound interest works

With compound interest you earn interest on your interest. Each period, the interest is added to the balance, and the next period's interest is figured on the larger amount. The longer the money stays, the faster it grows.

Balance = Principal × (1 + rate ÷ n)n × years
n = times interest compounds per year

Example: $10,000 at 6% compounded monthly for 20 years, with nothing added, grows to about $33,102. Add $200 every month and the balance is about $125,510, of which $58,000 is money you put in and about $67,510 is interest.

Monthly contributions in this calculator are added at the end of each month.

Frequently asked questions

Does compounding more often make a big difference?

A little. At 6%, daily compounding gives an effective yield of about 6.18% against 6.00% for annual compounding. The rate and the number of years matter far more.

What is the effective annual yield?

It is the interest you actually earn in one year after compounding, shown as a single percentage. It is what banks often advertise as APY.

Does this include taxes or inflation?

No. Taxes, fees and inflation reduce what you keep and what the money can buy. Real returns also vary from year to year, so treat the result as an illustration, not a prediction.
