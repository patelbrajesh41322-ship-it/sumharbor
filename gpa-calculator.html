How GPA is calculated

Each letter grade is worth a number of grade points. Multiply the points by the course's credit hours to get quality points. Add up the quality points and divide by the total credit hours.

GPA = total quality points ÷ total credit hours

Example: an A (4.0) in a 3-credit class gives 12 quality points, an A- (3.7) in a 3-credit class gives 11.1, a B+ (3.3) in a 4-credit class gives 13.2, and a B (3.0) in a 3-credit class gives 9. That is 45.3 quality points over 13 credits, so the GPA is 3.48.

Grade points used here

  • A+ and A = 4.0
  • A- = 3.7
  • B+ = 3.3
  • B = 3.0
  • B- = 2.7
  • C+ = 2.3
  • C = 2.0
  • C- = 1.7
  • D+ = 1.3
  • D = 1.0
  • D- = 0.7
  • F = 0.0

Schools differ. Some give an A+ more than 4.0, and some do not use plus and minus grades at all. This calculator also does not add extra points for honors or AP classes. Check your school's own scale for official numbers.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my cumulative GPA?

Enter your current GPA and the credits you have completed in the optional boxes. The calculator combines them with this term's grades, weighting each by credit hours.

Why does one bad grade move my GPA less late in college?

GPA is weighted by credits. The more credits you have already earned, the less a single term changes the overall average.

Do pass/fail and withdrawn courses count?

Usually not, because they carry no grade points. Leave the grade blank and the course is skipped. Ask your registrar if you are unsure how your school treats them.

Is this my official GPA?

No. It is an estimate based on the scale above. Your school's transcript is the official record.
