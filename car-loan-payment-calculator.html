Car Loan Payment Calculator

Estimate your monthly car payment from the price, what you put down, the interest rate and the length of the loan.

How the payment is calculated

The amount you borrow is the price minus your down payment and trade-in. The monthly payment spreads that amount, plus interest, evenly over the term.

Payment = L × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

L is the amount financed, r is the monthly rate (APR ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months.

Example: a $30,000 car with $3,000 down finances $27,000. At 7% APR over 60 months, the payment is about $534.63. Over the whole loan you pay about $5,078 in interest, for a total of about $32,078.

How to lower your car payment

  • Put more down. Every extra dollar down is a dollar you do not borrow or pay interest on.
  • Shop for the rate. Get quotes from your bank, a credit union and the dealer. Getting pre-approved before you visit the dealer gives you something to compare against.
  • Choose a less expensive car, or negotiate the price before you talk about financing.
  • Be careful with long terms. A very long loan can leave you owing more than the car is worth for years, which is a problem if you sell or the car is totaled.
  • Improve your credit first if you can wait. A better credit profile usually means a lower APR.

A simple guideline for what you can afford

A common rule of thumb is "20/4/10": put at least 20% down, finance for no more than 4 years, and keep your total car costs (payment, insurance and fuel) under about 10% of your gross income. It is a starting point, not a rule. Your own budget, other debts and how long you keep your cars all matter.

Remember that the payment is only part of the cost of owning a car. Insurance, fuel, maintenance, registration and parking are not in this calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include sales tax and fees?

No. Sales tax, registration, title and dealer fees vary by state and dealer. If you plan to finance them, add them to the vehicle price before you calculate.

What happens if I choose a longer term?

The monthly payment goes down, but you pay more interest in total, and you may owe more than the car is worth for longer. Try a few terms to compare.

What is a good APR for a car loan?

Rates depend on your credit, the lender, the loan term and whether the car is new or used. Compare offers from several lenders, including your bank or credit union.

Can I pay the loan off early?

Often yes. See how much time and interest extra payments could save with our car loan payoff calculator.

Is it better to take a cash rebate or a low APR offer?

Run both. Subtract the rebate from the price in one calculation, using the APR you would get from your own lender. Then calculate the price without the rebate at the dealer's low APR. Compare the total paid (payment times months, plus your down payment) for each. The lower total is the better deal.

Should I make a bigger down payment?

Usually a bigger down payment helps. You borrow less, pay less interest and are less likely to owe more than the car is worth. Keep enough cash for emergencies, though, so a repair or a job gap does not force you onto a credit card.

What does "negative equity" mean?

It means you owe more on the loan than the car is worth. Cars lose value quickly, especially in the first years, so small down payments and long terms make this more likely.

Are the results exact?

They are estimates. Lenders may round differently, charge fees, or start interest from a different day. Your loan agreement has the exact figures.
