How break-even works

Fixed costs stay the same no matter how much you sell, like rent, software and salaries. Variable costs grow with each unit, like materials and shipping. Each sale contributes its price minus its variable cost toward covering the fixed costs.

Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost per unit)

Example: fixed costs are $5,000, you sell at $25, and each unit costs $10 to make and ship. Each unit contributes $15. You need $5,000 ÷ $15 = 333.3, so 334 units, which is $8,350 in sales, to break even. To earn $3,000 on top, you need ($5,000 + $3,000) ÷ $15 = 533.3, so 534 units.

Frequently asked questions

Why are the units rounded up?

You cannot sell part of a unit, and selling fewer than the exact number leaves you slightly short. Rounding up makes sure you at least break even.

What counts as a fixed cost?

Costs that do not change with sales in the short run, such as rent, insurance, software subscriptions and salaried wages.

What if my price is lower than my variable cost?

Then every sale loses money and you can never break even. Raise the price or lower the variable cost. Our margin calculator can help you test prices.
