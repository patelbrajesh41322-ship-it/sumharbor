Margin vs. markup

Both measure profit, but against different bases. Margin compares profit to the selling price. Markup compares profit to the cost.

Margin = (Price − Cost) ÷ Price
Markup = (Price − Cost) ÷ Cost

Example: an item costs $60 and sells for $100. Profit is $40. Margin is 40 ÷ 100 = 40%, and markup is 40 ÷ 60 = 66.67%.

A 50% markup is not a 50% margin. Add 50% to a $100 cost and you charge $150, which is only a 33.33% margin.

Frequently asked questions

Which one should I use to set prices?

Many retailers think in markup (cost plus a percentage), while financial statements usually report margin. Use whichever you track, but do not mix them up, because the same percentage means different prices.

Is this gross margin or net margin?

Gross margin. It only uses the cost you enter. Rent, shipping, fees, taxes and wages are not included unless you add them to the cost.

Can margin be 100%?

Not in this calculator. A 100% margin would mean a cost of zero. Enter a margin below 100%.
