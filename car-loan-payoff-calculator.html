How the payoff is calculated

Each month, interest is added to your balance: balance × APR ÷ 12. Your payment goes to that interest first, and the rest lowers the balance. An extra payment goes straight to the balance, so next month's interest is a little smaller, and the effect builds up over time.

Example: a $20,000 balance at 7% APR with a $450 monthly payment is paid off in about 4 years 4 months, with roughly $3,219 in total interest. Adding $100 a month finishes it in about 3 years 5 months and saves roughly $674 in interest.

Frequently asked questions

Will my lender apply extra money to the principal?

Not always. Some lenders hold extra money for the next payment instead. Check your lender's online portal or call them, and ask that extra payments be applied to principal.

Is there a penalty for paying a car loan early?

Some loans have prepayment penalties, but many do not. Look for a prepayment clause in your loan contract or ask your lender before paying extra.

Why does it say my payment is too low?

If the monthly payment is not larger than the monthly interest, the balance never goes down. Check the balance, APR and payment you entered.

Does this include fees, taxes or insurance?

No. It only models principal and interest on a fixed-rate loan. Your lender's payoff quote is the exact amount to close the loan.
