How simple interest works

With simple interest, the interest is figured on the original amount only. Interest is never added back to the balance, so you pay or earn the same amount every month or every year.

Interest = Principal × Rate ÷ 100 × Time
Total to pay back = Principal + Interest

Example: You lend 10,000 at 5% per month. One month of interest is 10,000 × 5 ÷ 100 = 500. Over 12 months that is 6,000, so the borrower pays back 16,000 in total. At 10% per month the same loan earns 1,000 a month and 12,000 over a year.

The calculator works the same in any currency. The symbol only changes how the answer is shown.

Simple interest or compound interest?

Simple interest stays flat because it ignores earlier interest. Compound interest adds interest to the balance, so each period earns on a bigger amount. Many personal loans between friends and some short-term notes use simple interest, while savings accounts and most credit cards use compound interest. Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a monthly rate and a yearly rate?

A monthly rate is charged every month, so multiply it by 12 to get the yearly rate. 2% per month equals 24% per year in simple interest.

How do I find the rate if I know the interest?

Divide the interest by the principal and by the time, then multiply by 100. If 10,000 earned 1,500 in 3 months, the rate is 1,500 ÷ 10,000 ÷ 3 × 100 = 5% per month.

Does this calculator work with days?

It works in months and years. For a number of days, divide by 30 to get months. For example, 45 days is 1.5 months.

Is there a legal limit on the interest rate?

Many places set maximum rates for loans, and lending money as a business can require a license. The rules differ by country and by state, so check your local laws before you agree on a rate.
